Engineering MeadowHawk

Flying MeadowHawk safely depends on more than the airframe. The power system, Pixhawk, Jetson, camera, payload, and ground-station links must work together while preserving the pilot's independent takeover link.

Architecture

Why we chose a QuadPlane

A conventional fixed-wing aircraft needs room to launch and recover. A multirotor can take off almost anywhere, but it uses more energy in forward flight. MeadowHawk combines the two: four lift motors handle vertical flight, and a rear pusher is intended to carry the aircraft through fixed-wing cruise.

The Pixhawk 6X handles stabilization and navigation. A Jetson Orin NX runs the higher-level Python mission and vision software. The aircraft also carries separate pilot and ground-station links, a downward-facing camera, and a payload bay near the center of gravity.

  • Vertical launch and recovery without a runway
  • Fixed-wing cruise for longer missions
  • Waypoint navigation and search planning
  • Independent manual takeover and telemetry
  • Camera-based target work and relief-payload delivery

The fixed-wing airframe gives MeadowHawk the efficiency it needs after vertical flight. The fuselage keeps batteries, electronics, camera equipment, and the payload close to the centerline so that integration work does not turn into a trim problem.

Construction

July 21 proof-flight configuration

Physical H structure, X-frame control allocation

MeadowHawk's carbon lift structure is physically H-shaped. The flight controller uses an X-frame allocation for the four motor positions, a distinction that matters when motor direction, output mapping, and pilot response are checked before a flight.

Lift hardware
The July 21 proof flight used four DJI 4216 KV310 lift motors.
Demonstrated scope
Autonomous VTOL waypoint laps, not fixed-wing transition or the full competition mission.
Design evolution
The lift configuration changed after earlier motor-mapping and thermal investigation work.

Proof-flight battery configuration

Shared energy, separate power paths

The lift and cruise systems share one energy source, but high-current motor branches and regulated avionics power have different jobs. Keeping those paths organized makes wiring inspection and fault isolation much more manageable.

Battery baseline
Three 6S packs in parallel; the proof-flight parameter set was configured for 13,500 mAh.
Distribution
A high-current board feeds the lift and cruise ESC branches while avionics use a separate regulated branch.
Evidence limit
These records do not establish current endurance or the final competition power configuration.

Airframe and structure

Wing, fuselage, and lift structure

The wing carries MeadowHawk in cruise, while the carbon lift structure supports four motors for vertical flight. The fuselage holds the batteries, flight electronics, camera, and payload close to the aircraft centerline.

  • Wing-loading, stall-speed, drag, and energy estimates guided the wing and cruise design.
  • The lift structure is physically H-shaped; ArduPilot uses an X-frame control allocation for the four motor positions.
  • Printed, machined, and carbon-reinforced parts allow damaged sections to be repaired or replaced.
  • The aircraft separates into transportable assemblies for field setup.
Propulsion and power

Lift, cruise, and shared electrical power

The lift and cruise motors do different jobs. The lift system needs controllable thrust at low airspeed; the rear pusher is sized around cruise thrust, current draw, and propeller pitch speed.

  • The design uses three 6S 4500 mAh batteries in parallel.
  • A high-current distribution board feeds the four lift ESCs and the cruise ESC.
  • The July 21 proof flight used four DJI 4216 KV310 lift motors.
  • We compare calculations and eCalc results with bench tests and flight logs.

The DJI motors were an interim proof-flight set. The TDR selected T-Motor MN501-S KV360 motors and P18 x 6.1 propellers for later testing, but that combination had not yet been flight-tested.

Flight control and navigation

Pixhawk handles the safety-critical flight loop

The Pixhawk 6X runs ArduPlane's QuadPlane firmware. It stabilizes the aircraft, follows waypoints, and manages VTOL modes and return behavior. The architecture keeps those safety-critical flight functions on the Pixhawk instead of the Jetson.

Modes and missions

ArduPilot provides the assisted VTOL modes, autonomous waypoint missions, guided navigation, return-to-launch behavior, and landing logic.

Navigation and recovery

GNSS supplies position data, while return modes and the safety pilot's independent manual-control link provide recovery options. Airspeed and geofence settings are checked for each flight configuration.

Simulation and tuning

We use ArduPilot SITL, Gazebo, Mission Planner, short flight tests, and log review before expanding the flight envelope.

Mission computing and perception

Python runs the mission and camera pipeline

The Jetson Orin NX runs the team's Python software. It reads vehicle telemetry through PyMAVLink, tracks mission state, captures camera frames, and runs the detection pipeline. It does not replace the Pixhawk's stabilization or failsafe functions.

  • Python scripts coordinate mission states and higher-level commands.
  • The camera pipeline prepares frames and runs object detection on the Jetson.
  • Recorded-data tests detected tent and mannequin targets.
  • At TDR completion, live MeadowHawk detection and geolocation-error testing remained pending.
Aircraft communications

Separate links for pilot control and mission supervision

The safety pilot and ground-station operator have different jobs, so MeadowHawk does not depend on one link for every function.

Safety pilot

ExpressLRS carries the pilot's manual-control link and provides a direct path to take over from the autonomous mission.

Telemetry

A separate 915 MHz telemetry radio carries MAVLink data between the aircraft and the ground station.

Mission Planner

The ground-station operator uses Mission Planner to build missions, monitor position and flight state, and review logs after testing.

Ground Control Station

One field case for planning, telemetry, and video

The portable ground station gives the operator one place to prepare a mission, follow aircraft state, watch the camera feed, and review logs. It supports the crew without replacing the safety pilot's independent control link.

Mission planning
Mission Planner is used to build routes, inspect aircraft state, and review flight logs.
Crew roles
The ground-station operator supervises the mission while the safety pilot retains a separate manual-control path.
Situational awareness
Telemetry and video are presented together so the crew can compare vehicle state with the camera view.
Field use
The displays and supporting equipment travel as a self-contained case for workshop and flight-line checks.
Payload system

Retention, release, and delivery are separate tests

The payload bay is designed to hold two independently commanded relief objects near the center of gravity. Keeping the load near that point limits trim changes as the objects are carried and released.

  • MEP flights demonstrated payload retention and commanded release.
  • The release channels can be commanded independently.
  • Target-relative delivery accuracy on MeadowHawk had not yet been demonstrated.
  • The full detection-to-delivery sequence remained a later integration step.

Safety and verification

We move up one test at a time

New work starts in calculations, simulation, or bench testing before it reaches a flight vehicle. We keep MEP results separate from MeadowHawk results, review telemetry after each flight, and recheck the configuration before moving to the next step. Manual takeover, return modes, geofencing, and preflight checks are part of that process.
