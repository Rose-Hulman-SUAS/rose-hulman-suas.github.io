Development and validation

Test, learn, revise, fly again

The team uses simulation, subsystem platforms, bench tests, ground tests, and progressively more demanding flight tests. Problems and corrective actions are recorded because the development history is as important as the final configuration.

Early program development

Analytical design and major aircraft revisions

The team developed first-order models for wing loading, stall speed, drag, cruise thrust, hover power, and mission energy. eCalc and CAD studies were then used to compare motor, propeller, battery, mass, and geometry choices. MeadowHawk progressed through several major revisions as packaging and weight estimates became more realistic.

Parallel subsystem development

Multirotor Experiment Platform

MEP was built to carry the competition flight stack, camera, communications hardware, and modular payload system before MeadowHawk was ready. It supported autopilot tuning, waypoint tests, camera integration, competition-altitude image collection, and payload deployment.

Software verification

Software-in-the-loop and ground-station testing

ArduPilot SITL, Gazebo, mission scripts, and Mission Planner were used together to validate network connections, arming states, flight-mode behavior, waypoint handling, and higher-level mission logic before enabling equivalent behavior on the physical aircraft.

July 2026

Initial VTOL problem and corrective action

An early MeadowHawk VTOL test produced a persistent yaw rotation and uneven motor heating, and one lift motor was damaged. The team reviewed motor mapping, direction, frame configuration, structural stiffness, control behavior, and the flight logs. The lift arrangement was changed from an H-frame interpretation to an X-frame configuration, the arm and motor-pod structure was reinforced, and the propulsion setup was rechecked before further flight.

Engineering outcome

The follow-on hover and assisted-mode tests showed that the yaw problem had been resolved. The incident is retained in the public history because it directly influenced the final configuration and testing process.

25 July 2026

Approved one-mile proof-of-flight mission

MeadowHawk completed six approximately 900-foot waypoint laps for a total distance exceeding one mile. The demonstration used the X-frame lift configuration and four DJI 4216 KV310 lift motors. The proof video and supporting flight data were accepted by the competition reviewers.

  • Autonomous waypoint circuit completed
  • Required ground-station information displayed
  • Battery performance aligned with previous planning
  • Post-flight logs and parameters retained for review
