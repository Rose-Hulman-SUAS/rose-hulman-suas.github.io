SUAS 2025-2026 Competition Vehicle

MeadowHawk

A student-designed autonomous QuadPlane built by Rose Aerial Systems for rapid storm-response mapping, detection, and payload delivery.

Explore the aircraft View flight testing Watch proof-of-flight video
Proof-flight milestone: the team completed its approved one-mile waypoint demonstration in July 2026.
Current aircraft

Fixed-wing efficiency with vertical takeoff and landing

MeadowHawk combines four vertical lift motors with a rear-mounted cruise motor. The architecture is intended to launch without a runway, transition to efficient forward flight, search an assigned area, deploy relief payloads, and return for a vertical landing.

System overview

Designed across disciplines

The website is intentionally compact, but each area below summarizes a major part of the vehicle and links to the supporting engineering record.

Airframe

Fixed-wing aerodynamics, structures, VTOL booms, landing gear, and transport considerations.

Propulsion

Separate lift and cruise propulsion systems, high-current power distribution, and battery integration.

Autonomy

ArduPilot flight control, companion-computer mission logic, telemetry, and manual takeover.

Mission systems

Computer vision, mapping, target localization, and modular relief-payload deployment.

Risk reduction

Testing critical systems before the full aircraft was ready

The team built a Multirotor Experiment Platform (MEP) to test the Pixhawk and Jetson flight stack, camera, detection software, communications, and modular payload mechanism while MeadowHawk was still being manufactured and revised.

  • Competition-altitude image collection
  • Payload-drop and deployment testing
  • Autonomous flight-mode and waypoint testing
  • Early integration of the camera and companion computer
