Meet MeadowHawk

A five-motor VTOL fixed-wing aircraft developed for the 2026 SUAS mission.

MeadowHawk takes its name from the red meadowhawk dragonfly, found across the Midwest, and Rose-Hulman's red identity. The aircraft uses four lift motors for vertical flight and a rear pusher for its planned fixed-wing cruise mode.

The July 21 proof flight demonstrated autonomous VTOL waypoint flight. Fixed-wing transition and the complete search-and-delivery mission remain separate integration work.

Vehicle overview

A fixed wing that can take off vertically

Four motors handle vertical takeoff, hover, and landing. A fifth motor at the rear provides cruise thrust. The design combines runway-independent operation with the goal of retaining fixed-wing cruise efficiency.

ConfigurationQuadPlane with rear pusher

Four lift motors handle vertical flight. The rear motor provides thrust for the planned fixed-wing cruise mode.

Lift arrangementH-shaped structure; X-frame control allocation

The physical carbon structure remains H-shaped. ArduPilot's X-frame allocation matches the installed motor positions.

TDR design conditionApproximately 19 lb

This is the aircraft weight used for the Technical Design Report design condition, not a newly measured competition weight.

Energy systemThree parallel 6S 4500 mAh packs

Three 22.2 V packs operate in parallel as one shared electrical system for lift, cruise, avionics, and mission hardware.

Flight controllerPixhawk 6X

ArduPilot on the Pixhawk handles stabilization, navigation, flight modes, and recovery behavior.

Companion computerNVIDIA Jetson Orin NX

Python software on the Jetson runs mission state and perception while the Pixhawk retains the flight-control loop.

Ground stationMission Planner

The ground crew uses Mission Planner for planning, telemetry, mode supervision, and flight-log review.

MissionSearch and relief delivery

Search, target location, and delivery are planned competition capabilities; they have not yet been demonstrated together on MeadowHawk.

Competition mission

From takeoff to delivery

The planned competition mission ties together autonomous flight, a camera search, target location, and payload delivery, with the ground station and safety pilot supervising the aircraft.

  1. 01Vertical takeoff

    Launch and climb under QuadPlane VTOL control.

  2. 02Autonomous navigation

    Follow the planned waypoint route under safety-pilot supervision.

  3. 03Search and mapping

    Enter the assigned area and collect mission imagery.

  4. 04Target detection

    Process imagery and estimate the target's location.

  5. 05Payload delivery

    Navigate to the delivery point and command the selected release.

  6. 06Return and landing

    Return home and recover in vertical flight.

These are planned competition stages. MeadowHawk has not completed them as one end-to-end mission.

System overview

The aircraft is one system

Airframe, propulsion, flight controls, mission software, camera, communications, and payload work all affect one another, so the team develops them as parts of the same aircraft.

Airframe and structure

The wing, fuselage, tail, lift booms, and landing gear have to be light enough to fly and sturdy enough for field use.

Propulsion and power

Separate lift and cruise motors share the battery system but serve different parts of the flight.

Flight control and navigation

The Pixhawk stabilizes the aircraft, follows the flight plan, and manages flight modes and recovery behavior.

Mission computing and perception

Python software on the Jetson handles mission state, camera frames, and the detection pipeline.

Communications and ground station

Independent pilot-control and telemetry links keep manual takeover separate from mission supervision.

Payload system

The payload bay, release hardware, and delivery procedure are tested separately before full mission integration.

Risk reduction

MEP let us test hardware before MeadowHawk flew

While MeadowHawk was still being built, we used the Multirotor Experiment Platform (MEP) to fly the Pixhawk and Jetson, collect camera data, test communications, and work on the payload mechanism.

  • Competition-altitude image collection
  • Payload release and deployment tests
  • Autonomous flight modes and waypoint missions
  • Camera and companion-computer integration
