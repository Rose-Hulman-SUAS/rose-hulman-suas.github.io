SUAS 2025-2026 Competition Vehicle

MeadowHawk

We are building MeadowHawk, a five-motor QuadPlane for the 2026 SUAS Competition. It is designed to take off vertically, search an assigned area, and deliver relief payloads.

Explore the aircraft View flight testing

Proof flight - July 21, 2026 MeadowHawk flew six autonomous VTOL waypoint laps totaling more than one mile. Watch the video.

Vehicle overview

A fixed wing that can take off vertically

Four motors handle vertical takeoff, hover, and landing. A fifth motor at the rear provides cruise thrust. This gives us runway-independent operation without giving up the endurance of a fixed-wing aircraft.

Competition mission

From takeoff to delivery

The planned competition mission ties together autonomous flight, a camera search, target location, and payload delivery, with the ground station and safety pilot supervising the aircraft.

System overview

The aircraft is one system

Airframe, propulsion, flight controls, mission software, camera, communications, and payload work all affect one another, so the team develops them as parts of the same aircraft.

Airframe and structure

The wing, fuselage, tail, lift booms, and landing gear have to be light enough to fly and sturdy enough for field use.

Propulsion and power

Separate lift and cruise motors share the battery system but serve different parts of the flight.

Flight control and navigation

The Pixhawk stabilizes the aircraft, follows the flight plan, and manages flight modes and recovery behavior.

Mission computing and perception

Python software on the Jetson handles mission state, camera frames, and the detection pipeline.

Communications and ground station

Independent pilot-control and telemetry links keep manual takeover separate from mission supervision.

Payload system

The payload bay, release hardware, and delivery procedure are tested separately before full mission integration.

Risk reduction

MEP let us test hardware before MeadowHawk flew

While MeadowHawk was still being built, we used the Multirotor Experiment Platform (MEP) to fly the Pixhawk and Jetson, collect camera data, test communications, and work on the payload mechanism.

  • Competition-altitude image collection
  • Payload release and deployment tests
  • Autonomous flight modes and waypoint missions
  • Camera and companion-computer integration
